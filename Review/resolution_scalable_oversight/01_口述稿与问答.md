## 1. 60–90 秒自我介绍

> I'm an Applied Scientist at Microsoft, on a centralized evaluation team for Copilot and Office agents. I've been there since 2022. My training is in math and economics, then a master's at CMU. I don't have a PhD.
>
> Most of my work is about whether an evaluation signal can be trusted. Concretely, I build the pipelines that turn agent traces into rubric-based judgments, I've fine-tuned small judge models on labels from stronger judges, and I do the failure analysis that feeds rubrics, regression sets and, through modeling partners, post-training data.
>

>

Your team works on the regime where there isn't one, and where the system being judged is optimized against the judge. I haven't done research in that regime yet, and I'd like to.

## 2. 主研究故事（2 分钟）

**为什么选它**：它是你持续时间最长、与“弱 judge 给出训练信号”最接近的工作，并且自带一个研究判断（agreement ≠ accuracy）。它的弱点是细节证据在我这里只有二手记录，所以下面有三处需要你确认。SQLGlot benchmark 的证据最完整（公开仓库可查），但它是两天内、大量依赖 AI 完成的工程项目，放在备用位置更稳妥。如果你确认不了下面三处，就把两个故事对调。

| 环节 | 内容 | 依据状态 |
|---|---|---|
| 研究问题 | 小 judge 的分数能否作为改进 agent 的信号 | 背景文件 §2.4 |
| 为什么重要 | 大 judge 成本高；分数会进入回归集与 post-training 数据 | §2.3、§2.5 |
| 我的贡献 | evaluator 的 SFT／distillation、数据筛选、错误分析 | §2.4 |
| 竞争解释 | 学到 rubric／学到 teacher 的错误／held-out 太容易 | 我的整理；你当时是否这样拆过 **［CONFIRM］** |
| 方法与证据 | disagreement mining 找难例，改 rubric、数据、回归集；能观察最终文档或状态时用它，否则只算 proxy | §2.3；“student 对 teacher 的分歧”这一具体做法 **［CONFIRM］** |
| 结果 | 约 88% held-out teacher agreement；约 72% inference cost reduction（旧版口径） | §2.4、§2.7 |
| 局限 | agreement 不是 human accuracy；专家校准仍在推进；backbone、split 等细节未补齐 | §2.4 |
| 下一步 | 没有更强 teacher 时怎么办 | 你的兴趣，**［CONFIRM］** |

> The project I'd pick is from my judge work. The question was: when can a cheaper judge's score be trusted as a signal for improving an agent?
>
> We score agent traces against rubrics with a large LLM judge. That's expensive, so I fine-tuned a small evaluator on the large judge's labels. On held-out data it agreed with the teacher about 88 percent of the time, at roughly 72 percent lower inference cost on that workload.
>
> The tempting reading is "the small judge is 88 percent accurate." I don't think the evidence supports that. High agreement has at least three explanations: the student learned the rubric; the student learned the teacher's shortcuts, including its mistakes; or the held-out set was just easy. Agreement can't separate them, because both models can be wrong together.
>
> So I treated disagreements as the interesting data **［CONFIRM: between student and teacher, and between judges and outcome evidence］**, and used them to fix rubrics and build regression sets. Where we could observe the final document or state, I used that as the check. Where we only had feedback or follow-up behavior, I reported it as a proxy and not as a verified outcome.
>
> What I'd defend is narrow: a cheaper judge with measured fidelity to its teacher, and a clearer picture of where neither is validated. Expert calibration was still in progress.
>
> The limitation is the part I find most interesting. I always had a stronger teacher to lean on. And these scores feed post-training experiments, so the judge's blind spots are exactly what optimization would find. That's the step from my work to yours: what do you do when there is no stronger teacher?

约 280 词。**最好补一个真实例子**（哪一类样本 teacher 和 student 一起错，你是怎么发现的）。有它，这个故事就从“我知道这个区别”变成“我遇到过”。没有就不要编。

可能的追问与诚实答法：

- *What was the backbone, the loss, the split?* → 按真实记录回答；记不清就说哪一项记不清。**背景文件里这些都缺。**
- *Did you ever measure the student against human labels?* → 背景文件写的是 targeted expert labeling “正在与产品团队推进”，不能说已有完整专家校准。
- *What was your part versus the team's?* → evaluator 训练与评估侧是我；被测 agent 与训练基础设施不是。

## 3. 备用故事（约 70 秒）

事实全部来自仓库：`README.md`、`submission/REPORT.md`、`REMAINING.md`、`docs/grading_review.md`、`docs/r4_composite_audit.md`。

> A recent project: a 100-task coding-agent benchmark built from real merged SQLGlot pull requests, graded by hidden tests in a fresh container.
>
> Most of the code was written by AI assistants under my direction, so the question I cared about was whether I could trust the grader. Every task can be checked against 21 patches with known outcomes. An audit against a list of shortcut patches found a false accept: a patch that smuggled a `conftest.py` in behind an allowed file turned every test green and scored 1.0 without fixing anything.
>
> For tasks that combine several PRs, I ran an omission audit: revert one PR from the reference and see whether the hidden tests notice. Three requirements were not covered at first. After fixes, 70 of 75 are covered by an executed variant.
>
> Two things changed how I read the results. The strongest model solved 94 of 100, so the set is close to saturated. And 6 of 75 repeated task–model pairs flipped, so I don't interpret single-task differences.
>
> This is evaluator engineering with a reliable verifier. It isn't oversight research.

需要你确认的三点：

1. 这个项目源自一份 12 小时的 take-home（`research/00_research_input.md` 写着“作业要求”）。**能否对外讲、要不要提来源，由你决定**；不要说出是哪家公司的题。
2. “audit against a list of shortcut patches”是谁提出的：你要求做这项审计，还是 AI 自己做的？按实际情况说“I asked for”或“the review found”。
3. 被问到细节时：agent 用 mini-swe-agent 在断网的 Docker 容器里运行；判分要求全部 FAIL_TO_PASS 与 PASS_TO_PASS 测试通过；75 项子要求里另有 5 项只有断言层面的证据，没有执行过的遗漏变体。
4. 三个模型的名字照仓库写法：`gpt-5.4-mini` 32%、`gpt-6-luna` 81%、`claude-haiku-5-5` 94%；每个 task × model 只跑了一次，没有 held-out，75／100 的 prompt 由 AI 起草且没有人工复核记录。被问到就照实说。

## 7. 两个提案的口述版

完整设计见 [Practice/01_两个实验提案](../../Practice/resolution_scalable_oversight/01_两个实验提案.md)。两者都**未运行**，也没有查证过是否已有人做过同样的实验，不要称为创新。

**提案一（oversight failure）：批评者能揪出哪一类错误**

> The obstacles post argues that debate struggles on fuzzy tasks because the answer rests on many weak, correlated claims and not on one locatable flaw. But GeoGuessr differs from coding in many ways at once, so that mechanism isn't isolated.
>
> I'd take text GeoGuessr, which has ground truth, and inject one known flaw into otherwise good explanations: a false fact, an overstated cue, the same evidence counted twice, or an omitted counter-cue. Then compare a weak judge alone against the judge plus a critic, at matched judge-call and token budgets.
>
> The prediction: the critic helps a lot on false facts and barely on double counting and omission. If detection doesn't differ by flaw type, the hypothesis is wrong and the negative result has another cause. It's a static test, so it says nothing about what a trained proposer would learn.

**提案二（改进方法）：用一致性检查过滤 judge 的假接受**

> In the debate-training paper, the baseline policy hacks the judge by impersonating it or sounding authoritative. Those tricks shouldn't survive a meaning-preserving rewrite.
>
> So on verifiable math, I'd select outputs against a weak judge with best-of-N, then re-judge each accepted output under changes that shouldn't affect correctness: a neutral paraphrase, and a swapped final answer that should flip the verdict. Only stable verdicts count.
>
> The fair baseline spends the same number of judge calls on plain repeated judging. If the consistency filter doesn't cut false accepts beyond that, it fails. Then the adaptive test: optimize against the filtered signal and see whether false accepts come back.
>
> Best-of-N is a weak stand-in for RL, and I don't know how this relates to what your team means by consistency methods. I'd like to ask.

## 8. 三种复习路径

每条路径的产出是“闭卷能说出来”，不是“看过”。时间不够时从后往前砍。

**30 分钟（只保住通话）**

|---|---|---|
| 10 | 读 [Brief](00_Screening_Brief.md)；把 ［待确认］ 改成真话 | 三件事各能用一句话说出 |
| 10 | 出声讲 §1 与 §2 各一遍，计时 | 90 秒、2 分钟以内；没有说出表里“不要说错”的任何一句 |

| 5 | 选定一个提案和三个反问 | 能说出证伪条件 |

**2 小时（加上团队问题的理解）**

- 上面 30 分钟。
- 30 分钟：读 #4 的摘要、§1 贡献列表、§5.2 Limitations（[arXiv 2608.17776](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17776)）。完成标准：能解释为什么用 judge MCC 而不是 reward 来定义 reward hacking，以及字数限制为什么必要。

**半天（约 4 小时，加上能应对追问）**

- 上面 2 小时。
- 40 分钟：#5 的 §1（四个 key claims 与三个假设）和 §5.2 Open problems（[arXiv 2505.03989](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03989)）。完成标准：能说出 safety case 的四个主张各依赖什么。

- 25 分钟：#3 的 §5 与 §7、#2 的定义一节。完成标准：能说出“只看答案能判对、看了 CoT 反而判错”的实验是怎么控制的。
- 30 分钟：把一个提案从头写一遍，不看稿（问题、臂、预算、真值、证伪、推不出什么）。
- 余下时间：补主故事里的真实例子与缺失细节（backbone、split）。
